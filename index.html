    Model assumptions

    One household, one shared age timeline, one pooled defined-contribution pension and one fixed-rate mortgage. The optional two-person UK tax estimate splits taxable income equally. Part-time and retirement income tax uses annualised 2026/27 rates in today’s pounds. Tax bands rise with inflation in the projection. The remaining tax-free pension allowance is a fixed nominal limit.

    Work and Coast budgets use the take-home pay you enter, including any State Pension already included in your budget. Coast can use its own take-home income. The separate State Pension forecast is paid and taxed during part-time work and full retirement.

    Cash earns the nominal interest rate you enter after tax. Home value and other assets and debts stay flat in real terms and count towards net worth. The optional relocation sells the UK home, repays the mortgage and buys a Romanian home in cash. Any purchase gap comes from accessible savings, including a Lifetime ISA from age 60. Include other debt costs in your spending assumptions.

    Each scenario must cover every month to the horizon and clear the mortgage by then. The safety buffer increases the spending used to test the baseline start. The target search, projection and stress test use your stated spending. A horizon of 95 means spending is funded up to the 95th birthday. You can also require a minimum period of full retirement before the horizon.

    Romanian costs, pay and effective pension tax rates are editable sterling-equivalent assumptions. Cash purchase costs are paid before that month’s spending. Returns should reflect local investment taxes. The model uses a fixed real exchange-rate assumption and excludes treaty calculations, local tax reporting and separate pension access ages.

    The Lifetime ISA can fund spending from age 60. New investment contributions after the move go outside the UK ISA, following the UK non-resident ISA rules.

    Verify your State Pension forecast and private pension access age. The normal minimum pension age rises from 55 to 57 on 6 April 2028. Enter a scheme-specific age where applicable.
